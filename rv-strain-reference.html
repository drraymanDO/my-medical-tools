More negative = better function. Some labs use -23% to -25% as the normal threshold — meaningful heterogeneity exists across vendors/software. Vendor variability often exceeds biological variability — use the same machine/software for serial studies.

Size-independent: No Sex adjustment: Not routine Age adjustment: Not routine
Absolute linear measurement (mm) — not indexed to body size, despite known BSA-related variability. Men trend slightly higher than women; TAPSE trends down with age. The flat 17mm cutoff remains the ASE guideline standard regardless.

Calculated as [RV end-diastolic area − RV end-systolic area] / RV end-diastolic area — inherently self-normalized for size, similar to strain. Age/sex effects less well-characterized than for TAPSE.

Practical takeaway: Guideline cutoffs (ASE/EACVI) are single universal thresholds, not age/sex/BSA-stratified nomograms — even though biological variability is well documented in the literature. The most important principle in practice is trending the same patient serially, using the same modality/software, rather than anchoring too heavily on population cutoffs — especially for strain, where inter-vendor variability can exceed true patient-to-patient differences.
